# SBC OS V65.1.72 — Spanish-Speaking Sales Representative Launch Kit

**Purpose:** Prepare Successful By Choice (SBC), serving Miami-area small businesses, to test one remote bilingual English/Spanish sales representative. This is an operating playbook; it does not activate calling, change CRM records, or authorize payments.

## 1. Role brief

**Working title:** Bilingual Small-Business Sales Representative (English/Spanish)  
**Market:** Miami-Dade small businesses; initial focus on owner-operated service businesses.  
**Work mode:** Remote, Mexico-based candidate; agreed overlap with Miami business hours.  
**Primary outcome:** Qualified conversations and customer-approved, paid SBC packages—not raw call volume.

### Responsibilities
- Call only prospects approved for outreach under SBC's contact rules and campaign lists.
- Explain domains, professional websites, business email, hosting, and the specific approved package inclusions.
- Ask discovery questions before recommending a package.
- Record call outcomes and next steps in the designated SBC system of record.
- Follow up when permission and campaign rules allow.
- Confirm a sale only after the existing order/payment system verifies payment.
- Escalate technical questions, unusual pricing requests, refunds, complaints, and privacy requests.

### Candidate requirements
- Fluent, professional Spanish and English; can switch naturally between both.
- Clear phone presence, active listening, concise note-taking, and respectful objection handling.
- Reliable internet, quiet calling environment, headset, and scheduled availability.
- Comfortable learning a CRM and following scripts without sounding robotic.
- Willing to complete a role-play and a short written follow-up exercise.

## 2. Hiring post — English

**Bilingual English/Spanish Sales Representative — Remote**

Successful By Choice helps small businesses improve their online presence with professional websites, domains, business email, and hosting. We are looking for a bilingual sales representative who can speak with business owners, understand their needs, explain approved service packages clearly, and keep accurate follow-up notes.

**You should have:** fluent English and Spanish, a professional phone manner, strong listening skills, reliable internet, and the ability to track conversations and follow-ups consistently.

**The pilot:** a structured 30-day test with training, weekly coaching, clear performance measures, and written compensation terms before work begins. This is a sales role, not a promise of guaranteed earnings. Candidates will complete a short bilingual role-play.

Apply with your time zone, languages, relevant sales/customer-service experience, availability, and a short voice introduction in both languages. Do not send sensitive identity or banking documents during initial screening.

## 4. Interview and role-play scorecard

Score each area from 1 to 5; multiply by 4 to convert to a 100-point score.

| Competency | What to test | Weight |
|---|---|---:|
| English and Spanish fluency | Clear, natural explanation in both languages | 20 |
| Discovery and listening | Asks useful questions and summarizes needs | 20 |
| Value proposition | Explains the benefit without jargon or exaggeration | 20 |
| Objection handling | Responds respectfully; does not pressure or mislead | 20 |
| Follow-through and accuracy | Takes usable notes and asks for a clear next step | 20 |

**Suggested pass:** 75/100, with no score below 3/5 in honesty, listening, or follow-through.

### Role-play prompt
The prospect owns a Miami barber shop, gets most referrals through Instagram, and says, “I don't need a website.” Ask the candidate to:
1. Open naturally in English, then repeat in Spanish.
2. Ask two discovery questions.
3. Explain how a website can complement—not replace—social media.
4. Handle “How much does it cost?” using only approved package pricing.
5. Ask permission for a next step without making guarantees.

Reject candidates who invent discounts, guarantee Google rankings, hide recurring fees, pressure a prospect after a clear refusal, or mark an order as sold without payment verification.

## 5. Approved discovery and call flow

### English opening
“Hi, my name is [Name], calling from Successful By Choice. We help small businesses with professional websites, business email, domains, and hosting. May I ask how customers currently find your business online?”

### Apertura en español
“Hola, mi nombre es [Nombre] y le llamo de Successful By Choice. Ayudamos a pequeñas empresas con páginas web profesionales, correo empresarial, dominios y alojamiento web. ¿Cómo encuentran sus clientes su negocio por internet?”

### Discovery questions
- “Are you satisfied with how customers find your business online?”
- “Do you currently have a website and a business email address?”
- “What is the main thing you would want customers to do online—call, request a quote, book, or visit?”
- “¿Está satisfecho con la forma en que los clientes encuentran su negocio por internet?”
- “¿Actualmente tiene una página web y un correo empresarial?”
- “¿Qué le gustaría que hicieran los clientes en internet: llamar, pedir una cotización, reservar o visitar su negocio?”

### Price response
Use only the current approved price sheet. Explain what is included, any recurring third-party/provider charges, renewal terms, and what is not included. Do not promise SEO rankings, lead volume, revenue, or unapproved features.

### Close
“Based on what you told me, [approved package] may be a fit. I can review exactly what is included, the recurring charges, and the next steps. Would you like to review it now?”

“Por lo que me comenta, el paquete [paquete autorizado] podría ser adecuado. Puedo explicarle exactamente qué incluye, los cargos recurrentes y los próximos pasos. ¿Le gustaría revisarlo ahora?”

### Outcomes to record
- No answer / voicemail where permitted
- Connected — not the decision-maker
- Connected — not interested / do not contact
- Follow-up requested (date, time, reason)
- Qualified — needs website / business email / domain / hosting
- Appointment or package review booked
- Order started — not yet paid
- Paid sale verified in existing order/payment system
- Complaint, wrong number, or suppression request

A “do not contact” request must be recorded and honored according to SBC's approved suppression procedure. Do not keep calling simply because a prospect is in a lead list.

## 6. Thirty-day launch plan

### Days 1–3: Setup
- Sign written agreement and confirm lawful pay structure, worker classification, schedule, currency, payment timing, and commission rules.
- Verify the approved package/price sheet, offer terms, recurring charges, refund/cancellation path, and escalation contacts.
- Train on privacy, accurate claims, approved contact lists, opt-outs, call-time rules, and recordkeeping.
- Practice scripts in both languages; no live prospect calls until approved.

### Days 4–5: Certification
- Complete three English and three Spanish role-plays.
- Demonstrate accurate CRM notes and correct handling of “not interested” and do-not-contact requests.
- Pass the interview rubric; coach or stop if critical accuracy/compliance gaps remain.

### Week 2: Controlled pilot
- Start with a small, verified prospect batch and daily review.
- Review conversations, dispositions, follow-up quality, and package accuracy—not just dials.
- Fix list quality and script issues before increasing volume.

### Week 3: Optimize
- Compare results by language, business category, and lead source.
- Focus on qualified owner conversations, appointments, completed follow-ups, and verified paid sales.
- Review objections and revise only approved scripts.

### Week 4: Decision
- Reconcile each claimed sale to an actual paid order.
- Calculate cost per qualified conversation, cost per paid customer, collected revenue, and gross profit after fulfillment/provider costs and commissions.
- Decide: continue, retrain for a defined period, or stop. Do not scale based only on calls or appointments.

## 7. Pilot dashboard and KPI definitions

Record daily:
- Attempts
- Live conversations with an owner/decision-maker
- Qualified prospects
- Follow-ups due and completed
- Appointments/package reviews booked and held
- Orders started
- Paid orders verified
- Collected revenue
- Refunds/cancellations and gross profit where known
- Complaints, opt-outs, and data-quality errors

**Definitions matter:** a lead is qualified only when it fits the target market and has a relevant need; an appointment counts only when scheduled with a decision-maker; a sale counts only when the existing order/payment system confirms payment. Keep attempts and outcomes separate.

Review weekly:
- Owner conversations ÷ attempts
- Qualified prospects ÷ owner conversations
- Held appointments ÷ booked appointments
- Verified paid orders ÷ qualified prospects
- Cost per verified paid customer
- Gross profit after fulfillment and variable selling costs
- Follow-ups completed on time
- Compliance/accuracy incidents

Do not set an aggressive close-rate promise before the first clean pilot cohort exists. Use the first 50–100 properly worked prospects to establish a baseline, then set an evidence-based target.

## 8. Budget and compensation guardrails

The working ceiling is **$500 per month**, but that ceiling is a planning constraint—not proof that any pay arrangement is lawful, fair, or commercially viable.

Draft budget envelope for discussion:
- Fixed compensation allocation: $250
- Commission pool: up to $150
- Tools/connectivity allocation: up to $50
- Contingency: $50
- Total planned ceiling: $500

Before offering the role, check local employment/contractor requirements and applicable rules for cross-border work, minimum pay, working hours, taxes, social contributions, and written agreements. Do not use a contractor label to avoid obligations that actually apply. If compliant fixed compensation cannot fit the budget, reduce hours/scope or raise the budget rather than relying on uncertain commissions.

If commission is used, write down:
- Which package/sale qualifies
- Whether commission is based on collected funds, and how refunds/cancellations are handled
- When commissions are calculated and paid
- How disputes and CRM attribution are resolved
- Whether recurring renewals are excluded or included
- Who approves exceptions

The existing UI's illustrative $15/$25/$45 commissions for $199/$349/$599 packages are **draft planning examples only**. Approve or replace them in a signed compensation plan before recruiting; do not represent them as final policy.

## 9. Contact, privacy, and claims controls

Before any outbound campaign, have SBC approve a written contact policy covering the source and permitted use of each list, applicable U.S. federal/state and Mexican requirements, do-not-call and opt-out handling, allowed calling hours, caller identification, consent where required, recording/transcription rules, retention, and complaint escalation. Cross-border B2B outreach is not automatically exempt from every rule. Obtain qualified compliance advice for the exact campaign and technology before launch.

- Use only authorized prospect data and the minimum information needed.
- Never export customer data to personal spreadsheets or messaging accounts.
- Never collect card numbers in call notes or chat; direct customers to the approved payment flow.
- Never mark an order paid based on a verbal promise or screenshot alone.
- Never claim guaranteed rankings, guaranteed leads, or guaranteed revenue.
- Escalate requests to delete data, complaints, billing disputes, and security concerns.
- Do not record calls unless the approved policy and applicable law permit it.

## 10. System-of-record boundary and go-live checklist

The V65.1.71 Sales Operations panel's scorecard is browser-local and is not a shared team CRM. Until a tenant-aware server integration is implemented and tested:
- Use the existing SBC CRM for lead assignment, call outcomes, and follow-ups where those features are available.
- Use existing order/payment records as the source of truth for sales and collected revenue.
- Use the panel only as a temporary planning/summary aid; export snapshots for review and store them only in an approved business location.
- Do not enter sensitive customer data into the local notes field.
- Do not treat a scorecard entry as a commission entitlement.

**Go-live checklist**
- [ ] Approved current package/price sheet and written compensation terms
- [ ] Candidate passed bilingual role-play and accuracy checks
- [ ] Access is least-privilege and tied to the representative's own account
- [ ] Prospect source and contact permissions reviewed
- [ ] Opt-out/suppression procedure tested
- [ ] CRM note and follow-up workflow demonstrated
- [ ] Payment verification and refund escalation demonstrated
- [ ] Daily 10-minute review and weekly KPI review scheduled
- [ ] Test completed without changing production customer/order/payment data
- [ ] Owner signs off before the first live calling session
